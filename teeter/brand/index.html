Identity standard · Issue A · 7 October 2026

We find the exact condition where your robot falls.

Teeter tests learned robot control policies in simulation. It searches a declared space of physical conditions for the ones that make a policy break a rule you wrote, then reduces each one to the smallest condition that still breaks it.

This standard sets how Teeter looks, writes and draws. Most of it follows from a single number.

35°

The angle at which a 7 × 10 block falls off its corner. Also the failure threshold, tilt_deg > 35.0, in Teeter's published campaign.

TT-001 · Symbol construction

The symbol is the sensor a robot balances by.

  1. A gyroscope: a needle that tapers to a point at each end, inside a gimbal ring.
  2. The needle stands 12° off vertical. Upright, it would be a compass; tipped, it is something keeping its balance.
  3. The ring is an ellipse tilted −24°, thick at its sides and thin where it turns away from you.
  4. The needle passes in front of the ring at the bottom and behind it at the top. Two masks cut the gaps; without them the symbol goes flat.

Line key · after ISO 128

Thick continuous: the needle, the ring.
Thin continuous: dimensions, extension lines, hatching.
Long-dashed double-dotted: the vertical the needle has left.

TT-003 · Wordmark

Machined, not typeset.

TEETER is drawn from rectangles on a cap height of 100. Every stroke is 16; stems are 18, a little heavier so the two read as one weight.

A stencil cut of 6 separates every bar from its stem, the way lettering is cut into a plate. The middle arm of each E is shorter; the R's leg stands free of its bowl.

Letters sit 26 apart. It is never set in a font, and never in lower case.

Horizontal lockup · primary

The symbol is centred on the wordmark's cap height, 1.7 times as tall. Symbol to name: half the cap height.

Stacked lockup

For square spaces: an avatar, a sticker, the cover of a report.

TT-004 · Tone

No colour. Ten steps from graphite to vellum.

Select a step to copy its value.

Text uses three steps only: Vellum 100, Graphite 300 and Graphite 400. Each clears 5.2 : 1 against every ground on this sheet.

Graphite 500 and darker are for lines and planes, never for words.

Colour on a dark page reads as decoration. Teeter shows failure with structure instead, which survives a black-and-white print of a safety appendix.

Emphasis is inversion. The one element on a page that must not be missed is set Graphite 950 on Vellum 100.

TT-005 · The hatch

Hatched means failed. Nothing else is hatched.

On a technical drawing, hatching marks a surface that has been cut. In Teeter it marks the part of a space where a policy failed.

The angle is the mark's angle. Lines rise to the right at 35°, 6 px apart on screen and 1 mm apart in print, 1 px or 0.18 mm thick.

TT-007 · Drawing system

The conventions of a technical drawing, used straight.

Thick continuous · 2 pxOutlines of the thing itself: the block, the ground, a robot's body.
Thin continuous · 1 pxDimensions, extension lines, leaders, hatching, axes.
Long-dashed double-dotted · 1 pxCentroidal lines, and the other positions of a part that moves.
Dashed thin · 1 pxLimits and thresholds, such as tilt_deg > 35.0.

Dimension

  • 1The value, in B612 Mono, above the line and read from the bottom or the right.
  • 2Dimension line, thin, parallel to what it measures.
  • 3Arrowhead, filled, closed against the extension line.
  • 4Extension line, thin, starting just clear of the part.

TT-009 · Voice

Say what happened, in units. Never say it is safe.

Teeter can show that a policy fails under a condition. It cannot show that a policy does not fail. Every sentence we write has to survive someone hostile re-running it.

Not

Teeter makes your robot safe.

Instead

Teeter found 2 conditions under which your policy breached tilt_deg > 35.0.

Finding failures is evidence. Finding none is not.
Not

A 41% failure rate.

Instead

41.1% of directed samples violated a predicate.

That describes the search, not the robot.
Not

A push of 7.875 N·s.

Instead

A requested push of 7.875 N·s.

At the default push time, the control grid delivers 20% more impulse than requested.
Not

Certified. Validated. Compliant.

Instead

Reproducible. Every result re-runs from its seed.

Nobody can yet say what satisfies a notified body.
