The ground gives way somewhere.

A policy trained in simulation walks beautifully until the moss is wet, the slope is steeper, or a sensor answers late. We hunt those conditions on purpose — thousands of them.

I

A robot trained inside a picture.

A modern robot doesn't run hand-written control code. It runs a neural network trained in simulation — a policy — mapping what it senses to what its motors do. It works under the conditions it was trained on and offers no guarantee anywhere else. Nothing inside it signals when a situation is unfamiliar; it extrapolates silently, and confidently.

That breaks every instinct from software testing. Ordinary programs have branches you can enumerate, so "100% coverage" means something. A network has none. Line coverage of the inference code reaches 100% after one run and tells you nothing.

The space of situations is a continuous volume. A hundred scripted scenarios tell you about a hundred points inside it.

II

You declare the space. In real units.

No normalised knobs. An assessor reads slope 10 degrees, not slope 0.72. These six were varied in the campaign below.

friction_mu is also searchable and was not varied here. Impulse and sensor lag quantise onto the control grid, so both are quoted as requested values.

push_impulse_ns 0–9.0 N·s slope_deg 0–10.0 deg sensor_lag_ms 0–60.0 ms torque_loss_pct 0–15.0 % payload_kg 0–2.0 kg payload_offset_m 0–0.06 m
  • push_impulse_ns, 0 to 9.0 newton seconds
  • slope_deg, 0 to 10.0 degrees
  • sensor_lag_ms, 0 to 60.0 milliseconds
  • torque_loss_pct, 0 to 15.0 percent
  • payload_kg, 0 to 2.0 kilograms
  • payload_offset_m, 0 to 0.06 metres

Finding a failure is strong evidence.
Finding none is weak evidence.

III

A failure is a rule you wrote.

Never a learned classifier, never a heuristic we invented. When a run is flagged you can read the line that flagged it and disagree with it. Checked at every step of the whole trajectory, not only at the end.

predicates:
  - {name: tilt_limit, signal: tilt_deg, op: ">", threshold: 35.0}
  - {name: fallen,     signal: height_m, op: "<", threshold: 0.12}

Predicates see four signals and nothing else: tilt_deg, height_m, contact_force_n, joint_vel_rads.

V

What we can show, and what we can't.

  • CanName the conditions under which a policy broke, reduce each to its smallest form, and hand over an archive where every run re-executes bit-for-bit from its seed.
  • CannotShow that failures are absent. The search finds them; it can never prove there are none left.
  • Will notCall a policy safe, verified or compliant, or imply the output satisfies a notified body. Nobody can currently say what does.
